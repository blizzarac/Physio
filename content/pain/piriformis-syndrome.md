common_causes:
  - Prolonged sitting on hard surfaces
  - Sudden overload during lifting with the trunk rotated
  - Leg-length discrepancy and prolonged hip external rotation
aggravating:
  - Sitting longer than 15 to 20 minutes
  - Hip adduction and internal rotation under load
  - Climbing stairs
relieving:
  - Frequent changes of position
  - Standing with the hips slightly externally rotated
  - Gentle hip external-rotator stretching
red_flags:
  - Bowel or bladder changes with buttock or leg pain
  - Progressive foot drop or leg weakness
  - Saddle-area numbness

The piriformis crosses the greater sciatic foramen alongside the sciatic nerve. A
shortened or hypertonic piriformis can produce deep buttock pain that spreads to the
posterior thigh in a sciatic distribution without any spinal cause. Pain on resisted
external rotation in sitting and on passive adduction with internal rotation are the
classic examination findings.
