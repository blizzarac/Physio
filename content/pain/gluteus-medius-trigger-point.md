common_causes:
  - Prolonged standing on one leg or asymmetrical weight-bearing
  - Sudden increase in running or hill-walking volume
  - Sitting on a wallet or on one hip
aggravating:
  - Walking, especially uphill
  - Lying on the affected side
  - Sitting slumped
relieving:
  - Sleeping with a pillow between the knees
  - Warmth over the posterior hip
  - Progressive hip-abductor strengthening
red_flags:
  - Pain with inability to bear weight after a fall
  - Pain radiating below the knee with numbness or weakness
  - Groin pain with night pain in older adults

Gluteus medius trigger points refer pain along the posterior iliac crest, into the
sacrum and the posterolateral buttock, and occasionally down the lateral thigh. The
pattern is frequently mistaken for lumbar or sacroiliac pain. Hip abductor weakness
on the same side (positive Trendelenburg sign) is a common associated finding.
